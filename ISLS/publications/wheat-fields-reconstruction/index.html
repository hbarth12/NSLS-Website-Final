How Syria's Wheat Fields Turned Into the First Battleground Over Reconstruction

A battle over wheat prices could be the first political contest over who reconstruction will benefit.

May 21, 2026 · Al-Monitor · Agriculture, Reconstruction

When thousands of Syrian farmers protested the government's wheat procurement price last month, Damascus backed down within days. The reversal revealed how one of the first major battles over the country's reconstruction is taking shape in its wheat fields.

On May 15, Syria's Ministry of Economy and Industry announced it would purchase this year's wheat harvest for about $330 per ton. Within days, thousands of farmers gathered across Syria's wheat belt — from the northeastern grain-producing governorates of Raqqa and Hasakah to the countryside of Hama — protesting that the soaring costs of fuel, fertilizer and seeds required a higher price.

After a week of demonstrations, President Ahmad al-Sharaa approved an additional incentive payment of roughly $70 a ton.

The price increase resolved the protests' immediate demand, but the demonstrations were an early test of whether Syria's new government intends to preserve the state's historic role in supporting farmers and keeping bread affordable. As reconstruction unfolds, Syria's leaders must decide whether those responsibilities will give way to a different model of development. The wheat protests are the first indication that those choices will face opposition.
